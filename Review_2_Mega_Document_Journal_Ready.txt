CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION AND RESEARCH BACKGROUND
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1.1 Introduction to GLOF Hazards
Glacial Lake Outburst Floods (GLOFs) present significant hydrological risks within the High Mountain Asia (HMA) region. The acceleration of glacial retreat, primarily driven by climatic variations, has resulted in the accumulation of substantial meltwater volumes behind unconsolidated terminal and lateral moraines. These natural barriers consist of loosely compacted glacial till and ice cores, lacking the long-term structural integrity necessary to accommodate increasing hydrostatic pressure. Rapid failure of these moraines can precipitate catastrophic downstream flooding, posing severe threats to adjacent settlements and critical infrastructure networks.

1.2 Research Objectives
Traditional methodologies for monitoring glacial hazard progression typically involve laborious manual digitization of satellite imagery or complex terrestrial surveying. Given the remoteness and structural instability of the targeted terrain, high-frequency physical monitoring is often unfeasible. Consequently, the primary objective of this research is to integrate high-resolution, multi-spectral remote sensing data with automated machine learning classification models and morphometric evaluation frameworks. This creates a systematized methodology to transition GLOF monitoring from localized observation to scalable, automated risk assessment.

3.1 Multi-Spectral Index Computation
Terrain element classification was performed through the calculation of Normalized Difference Indices, leveraging the varying electromagnetic reflectance signatures of the target topographies.

* Normalized Difference Water Index (NDWI): NDWI = (Green - NIR) / (Green + NIR). Administered to isolate surface water bodies by contrasting reflectance in the visible green spectrum with the near-infrared spectrum.
* Normalized Difference Snow Index (NDSI): NDSI = (Green - SWIR) / (Green + SWIR). Utilized to distinguish glacial ice and snow accumulation from surrounding barren landmasses.

CHAPTER 4: MORPHOMETRIC PARAMETER COMPUTATION
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

To evaluate outburst vulnerability, the mapped spatial boundaries must be quantified against physical dimensions. The structural characteristics were segmented into three distinct evaluation categories, as summarized in Table 4.1.

Table 4.1: Evaluated Morphometric Parameters
| Category | Parameter | Formula / Derivation | Influence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glacier | Accumulation Area Ratio (AAR) | Accumulation Area / Total Area | Indicates structural retreat and long-term ablation. |
| Glacier | Snout Index (SI) | GDEM slope at terminus junction | Highlights risk of kinetic ice-calving events. |
| Glacial Lake | Length/Width (L/W) Ratio | Geometric bounds of GeoJSON | Elongated lakes generate higher hydrodynamic tunneling. |
| Catchment | Catchment Compactness (CCI) | 0.2821 * (Perimeter / √(Area)) | Circular basins generate sudden hydrostatic spikes during precipitation. |

4.1 Glacier Parameters Computation
The primary kinetic driver of a potential outburst flood is the instability of the parent ice mass. Computations within this category prioritized the Accumulation Area Ratio (AAR) and the Snout Index (SI). Analysis of the AAR indicates the proportional health of the glacier, where values falling significantly below 0.5 demonstrate severe ablation. Concurrently, the SI values determine the localized steepness of the glacial terminus, quantifying the probability of massive ice block displacement entering the lake.

CHAPTER 6: ANALYSIS AND RESULTS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

6.1 Morphometric Correlation Analysis
Analysis of the integrated outputs reveals critical relationships between the varying morphometric categories. Specifically, a comparative review between Glacier Parameters (e.g., AAR and Snout Index) and Glacial Lake Parameters (e.g., L/W Ratio) yielded significant insights. Lakes exhibiting elongated basin characteristics (high L/W ratio) that were structurally situated below extremely steep glacial termini (High Snout Index) demonstrated exponentially higher vulnerability thresholds in the Decision Tree models.

The rapid decadal decline in the Accumulation Area Ratio fundamentally correlates with the physical expansion observed in the Glacial Lake parameters. As the glacier undergoes negative mass balance, the resultant meltwater accelerates the degradation of the ice-core moraine, which in turn acts as a positive feedback loop facilitating further dimensional expansion of the lake boundary.

6.2 Resultant Risk Distribution
The implementation of the comprehensive framework successfully isolated distinct threat variables across the 413 evaluated watersheds. By relying on deterministic equations and supervised Machine Learning classification rather than manual geomorphic interpretation, the project demonstrates a scalable, repeatable diagnostic approach capable of categorizing immense topographies against empirical data indices.

CHAPTER 7: TERRESTRIAL GROUND-TRUTH VALIDATION
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

To explicitly validate the probability metrics generated by the remote-sensing framework, an official field survey was executed across the East and North Sikkim topographies (Tista River Basin) under the NIDM-Funded GLOF Study between 19–28 December 2025. 

7.1 Geomorphic Baseline Observations
The expedition targeted the Tista River valley, documenting the infrastructural and geomorphic alterations sustained during the 2023 Chungthang GLOF event. Physical inspections observed severe riverbank erosion and extensive topographical changes. The ground-level severity witnessed in North Sikkim functions as empirical validation for the high-risk thresholds generated by the algorithm's Catchment Compactness Index estimations.

7.2 Logistical Feasibility and Future Operations
Observations in Lachung Valley and Yumesodong highlighted operational constraints, specifically that frozen lake surfaces during winter months blend heavily with adjacent snowfields, validating the reliance on rigorous NDSI parameters to differentiate solid terrain. Furthermore, the expedition verified target viability for future autonomous drone-based photogrammetry operations (e.g., Huang Valley, Sanglaphu Cho Lake), which will substitute 10-meter satellite limitations with sub-centimeter 3D moraine analytics in future deployments.

CHAPTER 8: CONCLUSION
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The developed methodology effectively validates the viability of autonomous disaster management analytical pipelines. Utilizing extensive temporal optics across a massive 66-scene Sentinel-2 batch dataset and historical Landsat-5 arrays, the implemented sequence successfully analyzed the geometric erosion of glacial mass and subsequent lake formation. The methodology acts as a comprehensive, highly scalable framework for mitigating the cascading dynamics associated with Glacial Lake Outburst Floods.
